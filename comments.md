**Content added or changed that is not in the draft**

1. **Week 3, direct cortical stimulation:** the draft's sentence broke off mid-way ("semantic errors and anomias are associated"). It now reads that temporal areas are mainly linked to semantic errors and anomia, and frontal areas to articulation.
2. **Week 6, sign language:** the draft said sign language was "only officially recognised as a full-fledged language in 2020". This is now specific: Dutch Sign Language (NGT) was officially recognised in the Netherlands in 2020.
3. **Week 7, phono-translation effect:** the draft said naming slows "when the distractor is similar to the target word". That describes ordinary interference. The phono-translation effect is when the distractor sounds like the picture's name in the other language, and the text now says so.
4. **Week 4, P600 example:** the draft said "the mysterious crime had been solving" has "a week agent". It now says the crime is an impossible agent for "solving".
5. **Small additions to explain or link content:**
   - Week 1: the closing sentence on inadvertent cues by trainers.
   - Week 3: the SydBat was developed to tell the PPA variants apart.
   - Week 4: a paragraph on combining methods.
   - Week 6: a sentence on what Levelt's model leaves out.
6. **New examples in boxes**, all standard textbook cases:
   - McGurk /ba/ + /ga/ → /da/
   - English versus Turkish "rolled down" gestures
   - Dutch "berg" for the phono-translation effect
   - Writing systems per type (Chinese, Japanese kana, Arabic/Hebrew, Devanagari)

## Changes across the whole document

### Structure (README §1 and §2)

- **Removed:** the table of contents and the Exam Analysis page. Week 1 now starts directly after the title page.
- **Title page:** rebuilt with the course name, course code, university, faculty and year.
- **Heading 1 per week:** for example `Week 1 – Introduction to language and communication`. Each week starts on a new page and has its own section, so the header shows that week's title.
- **White week box per week:** "Week N:", the intro sentence, "After studying this week, I know:", a ☐ checklist of the week's topics, and a purple pie chart.
- **Pie chart percentages:** left as given in the draft's exam analysis chart (week 1: 14.2%, weeks 2 to 7: 14.3%). Each chart shows that week's share against the other weeks.
- **Hook questions:** written new for each week, because the draft had none. Each is a grey `Hook Question` box at the start, with the question and options a–d left unanswered.
- **Answer boxes:** a grey box at the end of each week repeats the question with the options in the same order. It gives the correct answer, why it is correct, and why the other options are wrong.
- **End of week:** no checklist or pie chart. No "N" or "X" placeholders anywhere; the X marks in the Hockett tables became ✓.

### Headings (README §3)

- **Styles:** all bold "fake" headings became real Word heading styles. Heading 1 is Calibri 16, Heading 2 is 14, Heading 3 is 12 and Heading 4 is 11, all bold.
- **Levels:** topics are Heading 2, sub-topics Heading 3, and sub-sub-topics Heading 4. This follows the "chapter only" row of the README, since the draft has no lectures.
- **Video, talk and article sections:** moved from Heading 3 to Heading 2 and relabelled with an en dash, for example `TED talk – Deb Roy: The birth of a word` and `Article – Bilingualism in the early years (Byers-Heinlein & Lew-Williams, 2013)`.
- **Gorilla Koko video:** moved directly after "Can animals learn language?", which covers the same topic.

### Layout (README §4)

- **Page and text:** A4 with 2.5 cm margins. Body text is Calibri 11, justified, single spacing. The draft had 2.54 cm margins.
- **Header:** the logo alternates (odd pages right, even pages left) and the week title appears next to it on every page of that week.
- **Footer:** a line with the page number centred. Numbering starts at 1.
- **Spacing:** comes from paragraph settings only. All empty lines and manual breaks were removed, apart from the one spacer listed above.
- **Page breaks:** paragraphs and table rows cannot split across pages. Headings are kept with the next paragraph, and lists of up to 6 items are kept on one page.
- **Lists:** real Word lists (`•` → `o` → `▪` and `1.` → `a.`). The cognate examples and the conclusion lists in Week 7 were plain lines and are now lists or tables.
- **Emphasis:** all underlining was removed (for example in "Is there a desire to learn" and "b**oa**t"), and every body paragraph has bold key terms.

### Writing (README §5)

- **UK spelling** throughout, for example:
  - behaviour, recognise, organise, specialise, characterise, analyse
  - neighbourhood, fibre, centre, odour, programme
  - localiser, lateralisation, fossilisation, randomisation, memorisation
- **Long dashes:** all 6 em dashes (—) were removed. En dashes in body text ("Lesion–symptom", "Wernicke–Lichtheim–Geschwind", "5–15%" and so on) became hyphens or "to", so en dashes now appear in headings only.
- **"You" and "we":** removed from all body text, for example "when someone waves to get your attention", "we can create an infinite number", "we now know", "if we don't use motherese".
- **Banned sentence openings:** sentences starting with "There", "It" or "Therefore" were rewritten.
- **Linking phrases and AI tells removed:** "Furthermore", "Interestingly", "Importantly", "In the end", "Ultimately", "Language, any language, opens doors to the world".
- **Source references removed:**
  - "This chapter introduces…"
  - "This article reviews…"
  - "The authors structure their discussion…"
  - "In the article…, Pamela Perniss argues…"
  - "In her talk, Patricia Kuhl reveals…"
  - "Deb Roy's TED Talk explores…"

  Names and years of theorists and studies were kept.
- **Paragraphs:** very short paragraphs were merged or expanded to at least 4 lines. Long ones were split.
- **Repetition removed** (no topic was dropped):
  - The Matthew effect was explained twice (Week 7).
  - Lesion findings on aphasia were repeated (Week 3).
  - The head-turn procedure was described again in the Kuhl section (Week 5).
  - Semantic paraphasias were repeated (Week 3).
